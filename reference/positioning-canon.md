## 2. Canonical vocabulary — one meaning, everywhere

These words are load-bearing. They mean exactly one thing on every page, in both languages.

- **Outputs** — what AI produces: code, documents, artifacts. Quality and consistency are
  properties of outputs. The *Fundamental Principles* series (hallucination as mechanism, the
  value arithmetic, steering, backpressure) is the discipline of outputs. ES: *outputs*
  (loanword — consistent with his established loanwords: feature, steering, backpressure,
  harness, codebase). Typography: the loanwords are italicized only where the render path
  supports inline HTML (today: the homepage thesis bridge, where EN deliberately uses
  `<strong>` to parallel the block's bolded lenses and ES uses `<em>` per the loanword
  convention); everywhere else they render plain — amended 2026-08-21 after the parity audit.
- **Outcomes** — what changes in how the organization works and what it achieves: decisions,
  delivery, capability. Outcomes come from people using outputs well; an output is never an
  outcome by itself. ES: *outcomes* (loanword).
- **The bridge** — the site's central claim: consistent quality outputs are necessary but not
  sufficient; connecting outputs to outcomes is human, organizational work — and that is the
  work Aníbal does. This sentence (in some form) is the connective tissue between the
  Principles series and the services. It currently exists nowhere on the site. It must.
- **Impact / value** — what sustained outcomes compound into at the business level. Use
  sparingly; never as filler ("value-driven" is banned).
- **AI at the core of the workforce** — the destination state: not tools bolted onto the side
  of an org chart, AI woven into how people actually work every day. ES: "la IA en el centro
  de la fuerza de trabajo."

## 3. The refusals (they are positioning, not attitude)

- **Not consulting in the traditional sense.** No analysis-for-the-shelf, no reports as
  product, no slide decks, no readout theater. The strikethrough device (~~consulting~~ /
  ~~consultoría~~) is canon: it is diff semantics — `- consulting` / `+ facilitation` — on a
  site built from the terminal. It appears where the offer is evaluated (homepage services
  intro, About). The device only pays if the same block shows what replaces it: the four verbs.
- **The four verbs × levels × intentions:**
  | Verb | Level | Intention |
  |---|---|---|
  | Facilitate | organization, leadership teams | alignment and change that sticks — built in the room, not delivered to it |
  | Coach | individual executives | growth of the leader (contract → practice → autonomy) |
  | Mentor | engineering leaders, seniors | transfer of judgment — 30 years of pattern recognition |
  | Train | teams | capability: first-principles literacy, steering, backpressure |
- Existing copy already carries this instinct — keep these lines: "not a deck", "not another
  report", "no vendor pitch, no tool survey". The Diagnostic was the one service contradicting
  it; it is reframed (§5).

## 4. Hook hierarchy (decided — was three flags, now one)

1. **The flag (hero, homepage):** the offer — help engineering organizations adopt AI-assisted
   development *without breaking their teams or their codebase*. Stays the hero. Sharpened,
   not replaced.
2. **The argument (thesis block):** the bottleneck moves — AI multiplies output speed, not
   organizational judgment — now extended one step to carry the bridge: outputs ≠ outcomes.
3. **The signature (About + Principles):** "I help you turn AI into a boring technology"
   (Clarke, flipped). It is the intellectual signature, not the sales flag. At most once per
   page.

"AI at the core of the workforce" is the **horizon**, painted in the thesis and About — not
the hero. **[REVISIT]** ICP stays engineering organizations (the wedge); the workforce framing
is the horizon those buyers are walking toward. If Aníbal wants to widen the ICP to knowledge
work broadly, that is a bigger repositioning than this pass.

## 5. Services — reframed through the canon

The three-service architecture survives. What changes is what each one *is*.

1. **Diagnostic** — keeps fixed scope, fixed fee, 2–4 weeks, "evidence instead of demos."
   Reframed deliverable: the product is **alignment, not a document**. The diagnostic is
   facilitated: leadership builds the map of where the organization actually is *with* Aníbal,
   in working sessions — the written roadmap is the record of that alignment, owned by the org
   because they built it. Kill: "a clear-eyed report… presented to leadership" as the promise.
   Keep: the honesty register ("whether or not we ever work together again").
   **[REVISIT]** the name "Diagnostic" itself reads analysis-shaped; kept for continuity.
2. **Advisory & Fractional VP of Engineering** — stays, per Aníbal: *facilitating change is
   the core of how he incarnates this role*. Reframe: embedded senior leadership whose actual
   job is facilitation from inside the cadence — install the new way of working through the
   four verbs, then fade. The existing Plan → Embed → Fade structure is right; the copy now
   names the facilitation explicitly.
3. **Executive coaching** — already canon-aligned ("a confidential thinking partner, not
   another report"). Touch-ups only for vocabulary consistency.

Every service page: CTA directly under the lede, cross-links to the other two services before
the footer, and one line naming which verbs the engagement deploys.

## 6. Readers (EN and ES are different people)

- **ES reader (warm):** CTOs/CEOs/VPs in Latin America. May already know him — Platzi, the
  podcast, the essays. The ES site can assume more familiarity and lean on the public
  conversation. ES is the native language of the intellectual material.
- **EN reader (cold):** US/international buyer who found him via referral or search. Needs
  proof faster and cannot read the essays. Therefore the EN /principles/ page must carry the
  argument in English: credo, myths, and side effects get real English versions (they are
  short); the five essay titles stay Spanish (they are artifacts, marked `lang="es"`) with the
  English summaries that already exist.

## 7. Voice

- Dry confidence. First person. Short declaratives, em-dashes, concrete nouns. No superlatives,
  no "transformación digital," no AI-hype vocabulary, no "value-driven." (Factual third-party
  credentials are exempt from the superlative ban — e.g. Platzi as "Latin America's leading
  tech education company" is a claim about Platzi, not hype about Aníbal.)
- Terminal flavor is structural (eyebrows, mono labels, the strikethrough-as-diff), never
  decorative in prose.
- **Sibling rule:** EN and ES are written as siblings, not translations. Each page is drafted
  first in the language it thinks in (Principles material thinks in Spanish; service pages
  think in the buyer's language), then re-written — not translated — in the other.
- Loanwords in ES are identity: steering, backpressure, harness, codebase, outputs, outcomes.
  Do not "fix" them into Spanish.
- His humor is deadpan and self-aware ("I'm too old for this" energy) — allowed in About,
  never in service deliverable lists.
