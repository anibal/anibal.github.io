### Thesis *[the "why me" block — renders as a short, visually distinct statement]*

**The problem is still the people and the teams. It just got more complex.**

Software is knowledge work: humans making decisions together inside a complex system. AI multiplies how fast code gets written — it does not multiply how well an organization decides, coordinates, and learns. Inject AI into every stage of the SDLC and the bottleneck doesn't disappear; it moves — to judgment, to trust, to how teams actually work.

That's why I approach every engagement through two lenses I've spent my career sharpening: **systems thinking** and **building high-performance teams**.

I'm Aníbal Rojas. I've spent 30 years building and leading software organizations — telecom, oil & gas, aviation, and startups. For six years I was VP of Engineering at Platzi, Latin America's leading tech education company, where I built and scaled high-performance teams and helped hundreds of people grow into engineers and leaders.

Then I did something unusual for an executive: I went back to the code. The only durable way to work with these tools is **from first principles**. That's what my *Fundamental Principles* series proposes — written from the terminal, not from a conference stage.

That combination is what I offer: an executive who has run engineering at scale, and a practitioner who knows exactly what these tools do when a system is collapsing non-linearly trying to scale.

## Copy notes (for you, not the site)

1. **The thesis block is the integration of your framing.** It sits under the hero, not in it: the hero hooks with the buyer's searched-for problem (AI adoption), the thesis reframes it as a people/systems problem only you can solve. Leading with "the problem is the people" alone would read blame-adjacent and generic; coupled to the AI clause it becomes contrarian and credible. It's also the natural home for **systems thinking**, **knowledge work**, and **SDLC** — all three now appear twice (thesis + services/credentials).
2. **"From first principles" replaced "framework"** — and it's a stronger claim: playbooks expire with every model release; principles don't. The line "models, harnesses, skills, and plugins evolve too fast for any playbook to survive" is doing sales work: it implicitly disqualifies competitors selling recipes.
3. **"Collapsing non-linearly trying to scale"** replaced the tests line, per your edit. It signals systems literacy to technical readers in six words.
4. **The podcast earns a proof-point card**, not a footnote: a running public conversation is third-party evidence you can hold your own on this topic weekly. The name explanation stays — it's charming, it's identity, and it localizes you instantly.
5. **"Sin romper sus equipos"** remains the load-bearing hero clause; the thesis block now pays it off explicitly.
6. Deliberately still absent: "transformación digital," service menus above the fold, and AI-sounding superlatives. Dry confidence throughout.
